Validation

Unit tests check each function against hand-computed and published values. The validation suite runs the complete pipelines, the way they run in the app, and reports how closely MaveScape agrees with the reference, against a stated tolerance. It runs in continuous integration on every change, with the public data it needs fetched and checked against their SHA-256 checksums.

The same bits in every browser

JavaScript's Math.log and Math.exp may differ in their last bit between engines, and they do: Chrome 154 and Node 22 disagree on about 2% of logarithms. MaveScape computes its logarithms and exponentials with fdlibm's algorithms written in plain arithmetic, which every engine performs identically, so a run's output hash does not depend on the browser that scored it and a saved run reproduces after a browser update.

Methods it reimplements

MaveScape's scores are reimplementations of published methods. Their authors are cited in the code and in the methods paragraph MaveScape writes for each run:

  • Scores: log ratios of frequencies before and after selection, with normalization to the wild type, and for time series the slope of a weighted regression on time, as in Enrich2 (Rubin et al., Genome Biology 2017).
  • Replicates: by default, a shared error model of multiplicative and additive terms fitted to the differences between replicates, as DiMSum's (Faure et al., Genome Biology 2020), with each variant's dispersion moderated by empirical Bayes as limma moderates variances (Smyth, Stat Appl Genet Mol Biol 2004), degrees of freedom combined by Satterthwaite's approximation (Biometrics Bulletin 1946), replicates sharing an input combined by generalized least squares, and the rescaling anchors' uncertainty by the delta method; REML random effects (Viechtbauer, J Educ Behav Stat 2005), computed as metafor does (Viechtbauer, J Stat Softw 2010); Enrich2's own estimator; heterogeneity as Q and I² (Higgins and Thompson, Stat Med 2002).
  • Barcodes: a variant's barcodes summed before scoring, as Enrich2 (Rubin et al., Genome Biology 2017), or each scored against the summed wild type, as the Bloom lab's dms_variants func_scores by barcode, and combined.
  • Sorted bins: the weighted average of the bins' values and its scaling (Matreyek et al., Nature Genetics 2018), and the censored log-normal maximum-likelihood estimate (Peterman and Levine, BMC Genomics 2016).
  • DiMSum's model: fitness, the scaling of replicates and the error model of multiplicative and additive terms, as DiMSum 1.4 computes them (Faure et al., Genome Biology 2020), the error model fitted exactly by bounded least squares (Lawson and Hanson 1974).
  • Differential scores: limma's moderated t-statistics (Smyth, Stat Appl Genet Mol Biol 2004) on voom log counts with precision weights (Law et al., Genome Biology 2014), as mutscan computes them (Soneson et al., Genome Biology 2023), written from the publications (limma is GPL, and serves only as the reference in validation); the shared input cancelling from paired replicates' differences; q-values by Benjamini and Hochberg (J R Stat Soc B 1995).
  • Quality control: variance beyond counting read as DiMSum's multiplicative and additive error terms, and DiMSum's terms fitted to place a bottleneck (Faure et al., Genome Biology 2020).
  • Variant names and exports: MAVE-HGVS and MaveDB's columns (Esposito et al., Genome Biology 2019); a deposit's keywords in the MAVE minimum information's controlled vocabulary, as MaveDB keeps it (Claussnitzer et al., Genome Biology 2024).
  • Structures: PDB entries (Berman et al., Nucleic Acids Research 2000) and AlphaFold DB models (Jumper et al., Nature 2021; Varadi et al., Nucleic Acids Research 2024) read with Proteoscope's readers; the target aligned to each chain with BLOSUM62 (Henikoff and Henikoff, PNAS 1992), checked against PDBe's SIFTS (Dana et al., Nucleic Acids Research 2019); secondary structure by DSSP (Kabsch and Sander, Biopolymers 1983); accessibility by Shrake and Rupley's method (J Mol Biol 1973) with DSSP's radii, relative to Tien et al.'s maxima (PLoS ONE 2013); exposure and disorder by StructureMap's part-sphere exposure (Bludau et al., PLoS Biology 2022); contacts typed by PLIP's rules (Adasme et al., Nucleic Acids Research 2021); the scores by class by Mann and Whitney's test (Ann Math Stat 1947), adjusted by Benjamini and Hochberg's.
  • Calibration, for research: the ROC curve's points and the AUC with DeLong's interval as pROC computes them (Robin et al., BMC Bioinformatics 2011; DeLong et al., Biometrics 1988), the precision–recall curve's interpolation and area as PRROC's (Keilwagen et al., PLoS ONE 2014; Grau et al., Bioinformatics 2015), Wilson's interval (Wilson, JASA 1927) and Youden's index (Youden, Cancer 1950); OddsPath and its evidence strengths as the ClinGen SVI Working Group define them (Brnich et al., Genome Medicine 2020).
  • Protein changes: a nucleotide variant's protein change as HGVS describes it, computed as biocommons hgvs computes it (Hart et al., Bioinformatics 2015); nucleotide variants merged at the protein level as DiMSum merges them (Faure et al., Genome Biology 2020); MaveDB's mapped variants as GA4GH VRS alleles (Wagner et al., Cell Genomics 2021; Arbesfeld et al., Genome Biology 2025).

Privacy and security

MaveScape listens on 127.0.0.1 only, refuses requests whose Host header is not this computer and API calls from other web pages, and serves its page with a strict Content-Security-Policy. It makes no network requests of its own: public data are fetched when you ask for them (Public data), and the one other request it makes stays on this computer, asking whether Proteoscope is running, for the Structure view (--proteoscope off stops it). Remote control is off unless MaveScape is started with --remote-control; even then only programs on this computer can use it, and reading or writing files needs a token that only you can read. mavescape run and validate drive a private copy of MaveScape of their own, on a port of its own with a temporary library, which ends with them.

What MaveScape is not

MaveScape reports experimental functional effects for research. It does not classify variants as pathogenic or benign, and its scores are not clinical evidence on their own.
