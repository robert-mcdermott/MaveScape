Variant-effect scores you can check

MaveScape turns the variant count tables of a deep mutational scan into functional scores with standard errors, quality control that says when not to trust them, and an interactive variant-effect map, and keeps every analysis decision on record. It runs on your own computer as one self-contained program, installed with a single command: no account, Python or R. Files are analyzed in your browser and never leave your machine.

The GRB2 SH3 domain from the Human Domainome (MaveDB, CC0), one of the bundled examples, with one variant's evidence in the inspector.

Why MaveScape

Numbers you can check

Scores equal to Enrich2's to 5 × 10⁻¹³, replicates combined as metafor does, and the same bits in every browser. Each run is recomputed from its inputs when reopened and must match its recorded hash.

Says when not to trust them

Quality control reads depth, coverage, replicate agreement, bottlenecks and the controls, and every finding says pass, review or fail with its threshold and why it matters.

Private and self-contained

One program for macOS, Linux and Windows. Count tables are read in your browser; nothing is uploaded, and MaveScape makes no network requests of its own.

On the record

Scores in MaveDB's columns, the methods of the whole analysis with their references, and a certificate that computes every number again on another computer, bit for bit.

New to MAVEs?

Thousands of variants, one experiment

A multiplexed assay of variant effect (MAVE) puts a library of variants of a gene or protein through a selection, such as growth, binding or a cell sort, and sequencing counts each variant before and after. Protein deep mutational scanning is the most common kind. MaveScape starts from those counts, after tools such as Enrich2 or DiMSum have read the sequencing. It needs no MaveDB account, though MaveDB is where MAVE data are published. MAVEs in brief

What it does

From the count table to a map you can defend

Open the counts and the target's sequence, say which column is which sample, score, read the quality control, explore the map, and leave with files a reviewer can repeat.

Import

Count tables as your tools write them

MaveDB, Enrich2, DiMSum and dms_variants layouts are recognized, and a lab's own tables are mapped by hand once and saved as a template. Every variant name is checked against the target: lab forms such as A12V and _wt are read leniently and kept as written, and a missing count is never read as zero.

  • MAVE-HGVS checked against mavehgvs on 16,959 strings
  • Every problem that blocks scoring listed, by line
  • One file per sample joined into one table
  • Several MaveDB score sets opened together, a condition each

The design

Which column is which sample, said once

MaveScape drafts the design from the column names: inputs and outputs, replicates, time points, shared inputs found in the data. Check it in the Experiment view, where every column is accounted for and the design is described in plain words.

  • Two populations, time series, sorted bins and tiles; tables of barcodes with their barcode-to-variant maps
  • From a sample sheet, or DiMSum's design file
  • What each analysis can do with what is there, and what the bench or the protocol would add
  • The analysis package: counts, target, design and parameters, as mavescape run reads them
  • Each change in a hash-chained history
The design
The GRB2 example's design: three replicates, each an input and an output.
Scoring

Scores with standard errors, and the reasons for every gap

Log ratios with wild-type, complete-case, all-read or synonymous normalization, for a time series the slope of a weighted regression on every time point, and for sorted bins their weighted average or a maximum-likelihood fit; replicates combined under a shared error model whose 95% intervals hold the truth, or by REML, fixed effects or Enrich2's own estimator; eight ordered filters, each variant left out with its reason. Every run is immutable and kept with everything needed to repeat it.

  • Equal to Enrich2 2.0.2 and dms_variants 1.6.0 to 5 × 10⁻¹³
  • Time series by weighted regression on every time point
  • Sorted bins (FACS) by their weighted average or by maximum likelihood
  • Barcodes summed per variant, or each scored and combined, outlier barcodes found
  • 95% intervals that hold the truth 94–97% of the time, in 26 kinds of simulated experiment
  • REML equal to metafor 5.2 to 3.4 × 10⁻¹²
  • An Enrich2-compatible preset to compare with published scores
Scoring
Two runs of the GRB2 counts: MaveScape's defaults and the Enrich2-compatible preset.
Quality control

Whether the experiment supports the scores

Twelve findings, each pass, review or fail with what was found, its threshold, why it matters and the plot behind it. Variance beyond counting points to a bottleneck; dropouts, coverage, replicate agreement, outlier replicates and the separation of the controls are checked too.

  • Checked on simulated experiments with one planted problem each
  • Thresholds kept in the workspace, every change recorded
  • QC of the counts alone, before scoring
  • Two replicates, conditions or runs compared, the report first saying what their agreement can mean, then their maps side by side, their difference and the variants that differ beyond their uncertainty; another assay or a predictor too, put on one scale by their anchors or compared by class (Comparisons)
  • Each variant's conclusion checked against other reasonable analysis choices, the ones it depends on named (Robustness)
Quality control
GRB2's replicate differences vary 11× more than counting predicts: the bottleneck the Domainome's own analysis found.
The map

Every substitution, and what is not there

Positions across, substitutions down, colored on a scale centered on the wild type. Missing, filtered and low-confidence measurements are drawn their own way, never as "no effect". Click a cell for its evidence: the score with its interval, each replicate, every sample's counts and the sequence around it.

  • Tracks below it: domains, conservation, AlphaMissense, ClinVar, gnomAD and your own
  • Selections by click, rectangle, freeform shape or query ("missense at 40-80 with se < 0.2"), saved by name and combined, exported with their scores (Selections)
  • SVG and PNG export; the map described in words
  • Keyboard operation, with each cell announced
The map and the inspector

The structure

The map on the protein, and the protein as evidence

A PDB entry, an AlphaFold DB model or your own file, drawn by MaveScape itself. The target is mapped onto every chain that holds it, position by position, as SIFTS maps it, and each residue takes its position's scores in the map's colors; a selection on one is a selection on the other. What the structure says of each position becomes tracks beside the map.

  • The structures that cover the target, ranked; an experimental structure and a predicted model alike
  • Secondary structure by DSSP, accessibility, exposure and disorder, and contacts with partners, checked against DSSP's own output
  • The scores by structural class, and a test of whether the least tolerant positions cluster beyond what burial explains
  • Views saved with the workspace, its archive and methods; figure panels with their legend
  • Opened in Proteoscope, when it runs on your computer
Structures
GRB2's SH3 domain on 2VWF, colored by each position's median score: the core is dark blue, the residues that touch the Gab2 peptide are pale.
Figures

Figures that carry their analysis

A page at a journal's column width, its panels drawn by the views themselves: the map with its tracks, a structure view, the QC findings' plots, the scores, a comparison, a variant's evidence. Each panel says what it shows, so the figure follows a rescoring until it is exported. Exported as SVG, PDF or PNG, it carries what each panel was drawn from; dropped on the window, it says what has changed since.

  • Text set in points at the printed size; text under 6 pt and images under 300 dpi said
  • The same figure the same bytes, in the window and from mavescape export
  • Each panel's runs by their SHA-256, the QC thresholds and acknowledgements, the structure view's image
  • Rebuilt from its file, each panel compared with the drawing it records
Figures
A figure of GRB2's SH3 domain: the map with its tracks, the structure, a predictor's classes and the replicates' agreement.
The record

An analysis a reviewer can check

The methods of the whole workspace, written from the record with their references; checkpoints, and what changed since, variant by variant. A certificate computes every number the workspace reports again from its files, and anyone can verify it bit for bit; its review report is one file a reviewer opens in any browser, every number traced to where it comes from. Scores and counts go out in MaveDB's columns, and a run becomes a MaveDB deposit its own validators accept.

  • Every reference with a DOI checked against Crossref's record of it
  • mavescape verify: exit 0 verified, 1 differs (each number listed), 2 not checkable
  • The review report loads nothing, and checks its numbers against the certificate's fingerprint
  • Archives written the same bytes in every time zone; every file described in FORMATS.md

And

Built to be checked, taught and automated

Examples with known answers

Fourteen: three published experiments from MaveDB (two populations, a growth time series, sorted bins), five opened from it (published scores; a saturation genome editing screen; two conditions; a round trip; two laboratories' screens of one protein) and six simulated ones whose true effects are known, one with problems to find; each with its question, guided steps and what to expect, from a figure to a certificate.

AI agents and scripts

An AI agent such as Claude Code works in the open window through MCP, its changes proposals you accept or reject; R and Python scripts drive it with a function for each action; mavescape run does a whole analysis without one, the same bytes every time.

Accessible

Color-vision-friendly colors as a setting, WCAG AA contrast in both themes, and the map described in words and as a table.
